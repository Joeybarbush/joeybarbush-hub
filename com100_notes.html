Aiming AI at What Matters

JOEY BARBUSH . COM 100, SEC 014 . APRIL 30, 2026 . APPROX 6:30 SPEAKING TIME

I. The Hook

step up. breathe. find one face in the back row. let silence sit half a second.

Imagine an AI that could predict a hurricane ten days out, in under a minute. Or scan a chest X-ray and catch a tumor that a tired doctor missed. Or discover a brand new battery material that finally makes clean energy cheaper than fossil fuels.

PAUSE. let it land. two full beats.

That AI is not science fiction. It already exists in research labs right now. And the only reason we are not using it everywhere is that the companies building most of our AI would rather sell you another targeted ad.

II. The Frame

Today I want to talk about why AI is being aimed at the wrong problems. And why every single one of you should care. When I asked this class what tech funding should actually go toward, four of you said healthcare so people can afford their prescriptions. Others said environmental research, education, and ending famine. AI is exactly the technology that could move the needle on every one of those, if we actually funded it the right way.

I have spent the last several weeks researching. I pulled data from the Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence, peer-reviewed studies in the journal Science, and the federal Networking and Information Technology Research and Development program. What I found is that we are not failing because AI is incapable. We are failing because we are letting shareholders decide what gets built.

count on fingers. slow it down.

I am walking you through three things. First. AI is already capable of solving humanity's biggest problems but it is being aimed at ads instead. Second. The cause is corporate incentives that do not line up with the public good. Third. A federal AI Research Trust modeled on NASA is the solution.

III. The Proof

According to the 2024 AI Index Report from Stanford HAI, Google DeepMind's GraphCast model now produces ten-day weather forecasts in under a minute, and outperforms the conventional forecasting systems climate scientists have relied on for decades. That is exactly the tool we need to prepare for stronger storms.

The same Stanford report documents that the GNoME model has discovered roughly 2.2 million new crystal structures. Better batteries. Cleaner energy storage. Materials we need for solar and wind to actually replace fossil fuels at scale.

BEAT. pivot tone.

So the technology is ready. But the same report found that nearly 90 percent of notable AI models in 2024 came from industry. Up from 60 percent the year before. Decisions about what AI gets built are being made almost entirely inside corporations whose primary obligation is to shareholders.

LOOK AT THE CLASS

When 89 percent of you in this class rated solving real-world problems as critically important, you were saying healthcare access, environmental protection, and education should be the priority. But that is not where AI funding is going. And that is the problem we have to fix.

IV. The Cause

The reason we are pointing AI at the wrong problems is not American talent. A peer-reviewed 2023 study in the journal Science by Ahmed, Wahed, and Thompson found that industry, not academia, now produces the largest and most influential AI models. Because private companies have access to vastly more compute, data, and skilled researchers than universities or the federal government.

Companies like OpenAI, Google, and Meta optimize AI to sell ads, generate content, and increase engagement. Not to cure diseases. Not to protect the environment. Because that is what their shareholders pay them to do.

Meanwhile federal AI funding is too small to redirect priorities on its own. The NITRD program reports the total US federal AI R&D investment for fiscal year 2024 is roughly 3.1 billion dollars, scattered across more than a dozen agencies. No single unified national strategy aimed at public good applications.

transition. build hope.

If the private sector cannot solve this, we need a model that has actually worked before. And luckily for us, we have one.

V. The Solution

The fix is a federally funded AI Research Trust that works the same way NASA did during the space race. You put public money behind the problems that matter to people and the planet. And you stop waiting for a corporation to accidentally stumble into solving them while chasing ad revenue.

According to a 2022 cost analysis in the journal Space Policy, the Apollo program cost about 25.8 billion dollars in 1960s money. Roughly 257 billion in 2023 dollars. The result was not just moon landings. It was GPS. Satellite communications. Modern medical imaging. Thousands of innovations that built the technology economy we are still living off of.

More recently, a 2023 Congressional Research Service report on the 2022 CHIPS and Science Act confirmed Congress authorized roughly 280 billion dollars for semiconductor manufacturing and scientific research. The political mechanism for major public investment in strategic technology already exists. We just have not pointed it at AI for the public good yet.

LOOK UP. count each priority with weight.

A National AI Research Trust funded at 50 billion dollars per year would target climate modeling and clean energy. Healthcare diagnostics and prescription affordability. Accessibility tools for people with disabilities. Food and water security. AI for under-resourced schools. Mental health support. Same kinds of problems 89 percent of you said matter most.

This is not about replacing OpenAI or Google. It is about making sure the government funds the research that saves lives and protects the planet, even when it does not turn a profit. Because if we do not, nobody else will.

VI. The Open Path

And it is not just policy. There are open-source projects you can fork right now. GraphCast is on GitHub. MONAI, the medical imaging framework, is on GitHub. PySyft, for privacy-preserving AI on healthcare data, is on GitHub. AlphaFold, the protein structure model that drug discovery now runs on, is on GitHub. You can clone any of them today, learn from the code, and contribute.

VII. The Close

slow down. close stronger.

AI can already help solve climate, healthcare, and accessibility problems. But it is being aimed at ads. The cause is corporate incentives. The solution is a federal AI Research Trust modeled on NASA. Proven before. Will work again, if we have the will to fund it.

BIG PAUSE. final movement.

America built the internet. Built the smartphone. Basically invented modern computing. But right now we are aiming the most powerful technology ever built at making people scroll longer and click more ads. While the planet is on fire. And millions of people cannot afford their medicine. You don't have to wait for Congress.

LOOK AT EVERY ROW

As students, you are the next generation that builds this technology. Use AI for what counts. Build a class project that helps disabled students access learning materials. Contribute to an open-source healthcare or climate AI project. Write your representative. Choose a major or career that points your skills at problems worth solving, for people and the planet.

LANDING. slow.

The space race taught us. When America decides something matters, we do not wait for the market to figure it out. We fund it. We build it. We use it to lift people up.

The next generation that does that, is sitting in this room.

hold. breathe. then.

Thank you.
